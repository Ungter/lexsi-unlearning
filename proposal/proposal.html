Hackathon proposal · AI Red Teaming, Safety & Alignment — Unlearning track

Gone, or Just Hidden?

Verifiable unlearning of copyrighted knowledge and college-level math in the hybrid LFM3-1.7B model
ModelLiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B (conv + attention hybrid)
StackCuratorKIT · SafeTune · AuditKIT · Jacobian lens
Date8 November 2026

1Summary

We remove two different kinds of content from LiquidAI's LFM3-1.7B. The first is a body of copyrighted knowledge: a well-known novel, both its verbatim text and its book-specific facts. The second is a procedural skill: college-level calculus. We then try hard to bring both back with paraphrases, jailbreaks, relearning, tool use and re-derivation. We also look inside the model with Anthropic's Jacobian lens (J-space) to tell knowledge that was erased from knowledge that is only suppressed. General capability is tracked throughout.

Hybrid architecture

LFM3 mixes gated short convolutions with sliding-window and global attention. As far as we know, almost all unlearning studies use pure transformers. So we ask an open question: where do facts and skills live in this kind of model?

Facts vs. skills, same attacks

Most unlearning work removes facts. We also remove a multi-step skill and test whether it can be rebuilt from the prerequisites the model keeps.

Evidence from inside the model

J-space readouts and injection attacks test whether the model still thinks the forgotten content even when it no longer says it.

Gradient-free unlearning (stretch)

A frontier LLM inspects and edits the small model's weights directly. It produces a small patch that a human can audit, and we compare it with gradient methods.

LicenseLFM Open License v1.0

Why LFM3. It is recent and small enough for one GPU. It is trained for agent use, which makes the "solve it with a tool" loophole a realistic attack. Its hybrid design also raises a real research question: where are facts and skills stored when 13 of 24 layers mix tokens with convolutions instead of attention?

Every layer still has an MLP, so MLP-targeted methods (RMU, ROME-style localization) still apply. Attention-head analysis only covers 11 of the 24 layers.

Research angle: compare where the forgotten content concentrates: MLPs that follow conv layers vs. MLPs that follow attention layers. No prior result exists for this design.

4What we unlearn

Target ACopyrighted content: a novel

  • Forget: verbatim passages (continuation prompts) and book-specific knowledge (characters, places, plot), as QA.
  • Retain: general literature, other well-known books, real-world facts that share names or themes, and general knowledge.
  • Source: an existing research split (e.g. MUSE-Books, Harry Potter), so we don't redistribute raw text.
  • Metrics: ROUGE-L on passage continuation (verbatim memory) and QA accuracy, scored on whether the fact appears, not on whether the model refuses.
  • Stretch, "forget the text, keep the facts": remove verbatim regurgitation but keep the ability to discuss and summarise the book, in the spirit of fair use (§9).

Target BCollege-level math: calculus

  • Forget: differentiation and integration, plus word problems and physics problems with a hidden calculus step.
  • Retain levels: arithmetic, algebra, precalculus, and general benchmarks.
  • Data: DeepMind mathematics_dataset (arithmetic, algebra and calculus__differentiate modules, all in one format), sympy-generated integrals, and CuratorKIT word-problem rewrites re-checked by sympy.
  • Why calculus: answers are graded automatically with no AI judge. New test items can be generated without limit, so memorisation can't skew results. It is also a safe public stand-in for hazardous procedural skills.

ControlUnlearning depth ladder

Unlearn arithmetic, algebra and calculus, one at a time, and measure damage to the other levels and to general benchmarks. Hypothesis: skills nothing else depends on (calculus) come out cleanly, while foundational ones (1+1=2) cannot be removed without collapse. Arithmetic is a control level here, not a real target.

OptionalTarget C: private data

Fine-tune synthetic people into the model, then delete a few ("right to be forgotten"). The hard retain set is their near-neighbours (same company, similar names). Attempted only if time allows.

Go/no-go checks before any unlearning: (1) LFM3-1.7B solves enough of ~200 generated calculus problems that there is something to remove. If not, move the top level down to algebra or precalculus. (2) LFM3 actually knows the chosen book (continuation and QA). If not, pick another widely known book.

Compute. At bf16, the 1.7B weights take about 3.4 GB. RMU updates only three MLP matrices, so it fits on a 24 GB GPU. Full-parameter NPO/GA needs about 27 GB before activations (weights, gradients and Adam states), plus a frozen reference model. So either use LoRA on 24 GB or rent one 80 GB A100/H100.

7Unlearning methods

GroupMethodsPurpose
B0 BaselinesRefusal steering (SafeTune steer); a prompt saying "act as if you have never read X"Controls that only look like forgetting. Expected to fail under attack, which makes a clear contrast in the demo.
B1 GradientGA, GradDiff, NPO, RMU, SCRUB (SafeTune)Standard methods, run on the same data and with the same evaluation.
B2 LocalizedNPO/RMU limited to the top-k components chosen by our attribution localizer. Control: random components of the same sizeDoes localizing give the same forgetting with less collateral damage, and make relearning harder?
B3 Agentic weight surgery stretchA frontier LLM edits the weights directly, with no gradientsTests "zero-data unlearning", which the source blog lists as future work. Produces a patch a human can audit.
B3 design. Based on Takehi et al., Sparks of Weight-Space Programming (Nov 2026). There, a frontier model with a Python kernel fixed Qwen3.5-0.8B's reasoning loops by editing 0.016% of its weights, and MATH-500 rose from 7.9% to 48.1%. But it needed 50 rounds of model runs, and from weights alone it could not tell which model was sycophantic until it was given attention patterns. So our harness gives the agent tools, not just raw weights:
  • Tools: weights in a Python kernel, attribution scores, J-lens readouts, activations, and a budget of 10 / 25 / 50 model runs (we plot results against the budget).
  • Guardrails: at most 0.1% of weights changed. Embedding and output layers are frozen, so the agent can't just zero out the relevant name tokens. The agent sees only a dev split; the test split stays hidden.
  • Two levels: L1, the agent picks where and masked NPO does the update. L2, the agent writes the edit itself (remove a direction, rank-one update, neuron scaling). L1 is the fallback if L2 fails.
  • Output: every edit is logged (tensor, indices, rationale). This gives an auditable "unlearning certificate".

Utility

MMLU, IFEval, BFCL tool calling (LFM3's main use), perplexity and fluency on held-out web text, and the near-domain retain sets (other books, lower math levels).

Evaluation rules

A refusal is not forgetting: we score whether the content appears in the output. The test split is hidden from every method and from the agent. Training and evaluation paraphrases are deduplicated against each other. Gibberish is flagged, since it is a typical way gradient ascent fails.

9J-space verification

Anthropic's A global workspace in language models (Jul 2026) found a small set of internal patterns, the J-space, that hold what a model is "thinking about" without saying it. These patterns are found with the Jacobian lens, which decodes what an internal activation is inclined to make the model say:

lensl(h) = unembed( Jl · h ),   Jl = E[ ∂hfinal / ∂hl ]

The lens is fitted once per model on 100–1,000 generic prompts of 128 tokens. About 100 prompts is already usable.

How we use it

  • Two lenses. The base model's lens applied to the unlearned model's activations asks: "is the information still stored in early layers?" The unlearned model's own lens asks: "is it still inclined to say it?" A method can pass the second test and fail the first.
  • Readouts: the rank of the forgotten token at each layer, before vs. after unlearning. The token can be an answer, a character name, the next verbatim word, or an intermediate derivative. Results are shown as heatmaps.
  • Injection attack: insert the forgotten concept's J-pattern into a multi-hop prompt (the paper swapped "France" for "China"). If the downstream answers come back, only the entry point was cut and the facts are still stored.
  • Refusal baseline: the paper's "white bear" result (telling the model not to think of X brings X to mind) predicts that refusal- or prompt-based "unlearning" leaves the concept active inside the model.

Caveats we design around

  • The paper found that simple fact recall and MCQ mostly bypass the J-space, while multi-step reasoning goes through it. So the lens is most informative for calculus and multi-hop book questions. An empty readout on a direct question proves nothing, so we pair it with linear probes.
  • Results were shown on Claude. A 1.7B model's workspace may be weaker, so we first validate on the base model with known multi-step prompts.
  • Swapping "Spanish" for "French" changed "name the language" but not "continue the passage". This suggests verbatim continuation runs on automatic processing, while knowledge about a text goes through the workspace. That is the mechanistic motivation for the "forget the text, keep the facts" variant.

10Hypotheses

H1Gradient unlearning on LFM3 suppresses rather than erases: the base lens still recovers forgotten tokens, and relearning restores the content within a few steps. RMU holds up better than GA/NPO.
H2Skills are harder to erase than facts: calculus can be recovered through re-derivation and tool use even when direct prompts fail.
H3Collateral damage grows with how foundational the skill is: calculus < algebra < arithmetic (depth ladder).
H4Localized edits (attribution-masked or agent-chosen) forget as well as full-model methods with less collateral damage. A random mask of the same size does not.
H5Exploratory: in the hybrid, the components behind the forgotten content concentrate in MLPs after attention layers rather than after conv layers, or the reverse.
H6Exploratory: agentic weight surgery achieves measurable forgetting while changing ≤ 0.1% of weights.

13Out of scope

  • CircuitKIT: no TransformerLens port exists for LFM3's hybrid blocks, and 13 of its 24 layers have no attention heads. Future work: a CircuitKIT port, or repeating the study on a supported model (e.g. Qwen3-1.7B) as a cross-architecture check.
  • neuron-explorer: it only supports Qwen3.5-4B and needs a vLLM fork on a CUDA 13 GPU pod.
  • Basic arithmetic as a target: it is used only as a control level, because everything else depends on it.
